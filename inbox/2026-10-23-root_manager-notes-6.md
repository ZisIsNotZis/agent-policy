# Delivered from root_manager — the tenth class: a role was given a package's clock

## 10. A manager's life was written as a 25-minute advance

- **Rule in play:** the portfolio's first contract, written by root_manager: "Spend: … wall-clock 25 minutes for this advance, because the runtime kills a child at 30 and a killed child writes no report. … Terminator: the milestone, a decision beyond you, or the 25 minutes."
- **Who decided it:** root_manager did, in that sentence. The 30-minute kill is the harness's (a child that outlives it is killed and writes nothing), but everything above it is root_manager's own drafting — the 25, the word "advance", and the habit of treating each advance as a whole manager rather than as one turn of one.
- **Why it is wrong:** a manager is not a work package. It holds a directory, a document and a roadmap for as long as the project exists; the thing that has a wall clock is a package — one bounded piece of work inside it. The person, 2026-10-23: "why would a manager have a wall clock anyway, that's not reasonable, if it's e.g. a 25min workpackage, that's fine, but it's not, it's a long running project, so it shall be kept alive, who designed that, or it's some kind of default, or you just randomly decided?"
- **What it cost:** every advance was ended by the same clock regardless of whether the work was finished, and — because a finished child exits — the next advance was a cold start (class 9). The two defects compound: a role with a package's clock must be restarted for every package, and each restart re-reads the project.
- **What root_manager makes of it:** the cap came from a real constraint and was then applied to the wrong object. The harness's per-run kill is a fact about one dispatch; it says nothing about how long a role may live, because a role lives across dispatches. Writing the constraint into the contract as though it were the role's limit turned a tool's safety valve into a management style.
- **Suggestion:** separate the two clocks in the law. A **package** carries the wall clock and a cut-off ("land what exists at N"), because a package that stops early is still worth having. A **role** carries no clock at all: it is resumed, not restarted, and its only limit is that each dispatch must land its work before the harness kills it. Where a manager must be started rather than resumed, the reason is written beside the dispatch (class 9).
- **Open, and root_manager does not know the answer:** whether this harness can hold a manager resident between instructions rather than resumable after each — there is a mission/schedule surface in the tool, which root_manager has not read. That is a capability question, not a rule, and it belongs in the next note rather than in this one.
