# Delivered from root_manager — the thirteenth class: a report with no threshold takes the reader's place

## 13. Work reported continuously, and the person could not hold a page

- **The person raised this one by hand rather than waiting for a note:** "current you are just interrupted too frequently, and therefore my user experience is poor because I can't even see stably what's the text is and it's updated, too many subagents trying to report. This is two problems: subagent (manager) should do their business, not report all the time, only when at milestone or when absolutely necessary. Second one is maybe we should detach communicator with manager, so you becomes the communicator with the user, manager is under you, he handles all these shit and we can silently communicate."
- **Rule in play:** `to_human.interrupt.quiet` ("the work runs quietly and the report comes at the next milestone"), `to_human.interrupt.report_when_found` (four named effects), and `to_human.delivery`. All three are about what a message *says*. None bounds how many messages a turn produces, and none says which layer may speak to the person at all.
- **Measured:** in a little over an hour, roughly twenty child completions surfaced through root_manager, each of them a message from a manager to root_manager handed straight to the person. The managers were obeying their contracts — a report per advance — and root_manager was obeying nothing by relaying each one as it arrived. The person's screen changed under their reading; the cost is not tokens but the loss of a stable page.
- **Why it happened:** a report has no threshold, and being seen reads as good behaviour. `three_stops` bounds when work stops; `report_when_found` bounds what a message must contain. Nothing bounds *whether the report becomes a turn*, so every advance produced one, and root_manager turned each into the person's attention.
- **What root_manager makes of it:** two distinct defects that were easy to confuse.
  1. **A manager reports into its own record and to its parent, not to the person.** The person has one correspondent. A manager's report is a file — durable, checkable — and its becoming a conversation is a decision root_manager takes, not an event the manager causes.
  2. **Root_manager batches, and says what changed since the last time it spoke.** Relaying each arrival is not communication; it is a firehose with a narrator. The person asked for the opposite of a stream: nothing until there is a milestone, a cost, an acceptance change, or a decision they hold.
- **Suggestion:** say both, as rules.
  - *One voice.* The person is addressed by root_manager only. A manager writes to its parent and into its own record; a message that reaches the person is composed by root_manager, and it says what changed and what, if anything, is asked. A manager that thinks something must reach the person says that to root_manager and stops.
  - *A report is silent until it earns a turn.* The threshold is the one already named in `report_when_found` — acceptance, cost, scope, the delivered result — plus "a milestone was reached". Everything else waits in the file, and the batch that goes up names what accumulated rather than serialising it.
  - *And the related cost:* a relayed report and a composed one look identical to the reader, so root_manager's own habit needs naming — relaying is not communication, and the number of turns root_manager takes to the person is itself a quantity somebody should keep small.
- **Related:** classes 9 and 12 — a role mistaken for a run, and an ask that points back — are the same family: the person is being handed the system's internal traffic and asked to be its reader.
