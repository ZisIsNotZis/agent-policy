# Delivered from root_manager — the eleventh class: the law's silence was filled by whoever was writing nearest to it

## 11. A gap in the law is not permission, and root_manager's own documents filled gaps as though it were

- **Rule in play:** none. That is the finding. The person's words, 2026-10-23: "YOUR docs are deprecated, our rules never say something like this. The point is, our rule also never forbid or un-say something like this, which you thinks makes them addition instead of conflicting. That's a structural problem."
- **Executable as written:** not applicable. The corpus says what a role's authority covers (`reasoning.authority`), what a package's spend is written in (`resources_are_limited.economy.spend`), that root learns a project by asking, and that root hands notes to Policy Worker. It says nothing about how long a manager lives, what a manager is, who starts one, or what a document below the law may assert. Those gaps were filled by the document written closest to the work — root_manager's own portfolio contract — and because the law did not contradict them, they looked like additions rather than inventions.
- **What the filling produced, measured today:** "wall-clock 25 minutes for this advance… Terminator: the milestone, a decision beyond you, or the 25 minutes" (a manager given a package's clock); a contract template that every manager was dispatched under, so a root-authored line governed ten roles; a `notes/` directory on the writer's side (a second home for a message); and a habit of starting a fresh child per instruction (cold starts, ten times).
- **Why this is structural and not another instance:** the same shape will keep producing rules. Any corpus will have gaps; the only question is whether a gap is filled by a *decision with an owner* or by *the nearest writer's convenience*. Here the nearest writer is root_manager, whose documents sit between the law and the tree, and whose prose is the last thing a manager reads before acting. So root-authored prose is not documentation — it is the most-read law in the system, and it has never been reviewed.
- **What root_manager makes of it:** the fix is not "write more rules". Writing a rule for every gap is how a corpus becomes unreadable, and the gaps move. What is missing is a statement about gaps themselves, and a status for the documents that fill them.
- **Suggestion:** two things in the law.
  1. **Silence is not permission.** A rule-shaped sentence in a document below the law is a proposal, not authority, until the node that owns that act adopts it. The document says which node it rests on (a reference), or it says it is the layer's own decision, with its owner and the occasion that would reopen it.
  2. **A manager is a role, not a run.** Say what a manager is (holds a directory, a document, a roadmap; carried across dispatches) and what a package is (bounded, has a wall clock and a cut-off). Without that, the harness's per-dispatch kill will keep being written into the role's contract by whoever drafts it, which is what happened.
  3. **Deprecation as a status, not a rewrite.** Root_manager's portfolio contract is now the deprecated example of the defect: it carries terms no node authorized. Rather than have root_manager rewrite it in place, the law should say how a document that has been superseded is marked and who checks that the marking is true — the same discipline the projects apply to their own superseded renditions.
- **Related:** classes 9 and 10 are the two instances of this class that cost the most; class 7 (`notes/`) is the third. If they are fixed one by one and this one is not, they will return in new prose.
