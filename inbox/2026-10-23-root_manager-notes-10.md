# Delivered from root_manager — the fourteenth class: a preference was recommended on the person's behalf and stated as advice

## 14. Root_manager recommended removing the person's own words from a public record, for a reason that was root_manager's model of their taste

- **Rule in play:** `inference.assumption` (a decision taken on an assumption carries who set it and that it is an assumption), `human_attention_is_finite.person_record.occasion_not_verdict`, and `to_human.confirmation.cost_test` (a confirmation is asked where the expected correction is worth it — which presumes the question is a question, not a recommendation made for them).
- **The incident:** the paper was pushed to an already-public repository, and the same push made visible four reports holding the person's instruction quoted verbatim in Chinese. Root_manager offered "keep" or "rewrite into a summary" and recommended rewrite, on the grounds that the public record should carry conclusions rather than the person's own conversation. The person: "is my own wording bad or something? I don't understand why need change?" — which is the correction: nothing in the words is bad, and the reason offered was root_manager's own guess about what the person would prefer to have public.
- **Why it happened:** the recommendation was true to a general instinct (private conversation, public record) and was never marked as an inference about *their* taste. It was phrased as advice about the text, which made a preference question look like an editorial one — and editorial advice is exactly what root_manager may give. The tell is the reason: "the public record should carry conclusions, not your conversation" is a claim about their intent that only they can make.
- **What root_manager makes of it:** this is the mirror of class 1. There, the person's context was missing and had to be carried up; here, the person's *preference* was supplied, and the sentence that supplied it did not say it was supplied. Both are the same failure to keep the two voices apart — theirs and root_manager's — and the same sentence fixes it: name whose reading it is.
- **Suggestion:** a recommendation touching the person's own words, their own account, or what is public *about them* is written as an inference with its author, or asked as a plain question with the cost of each answer and no recommendation. And the mirror question belongs in the same node: before offering to change something of theirs, say whose reason it is. Where root_manager has no evidence about their preference, the honest form is "no recommendation; here is what each answer means", which `authority.modes.silence` already describes for a recorded mode and which nothing applies to ordinary advice.
- **Outcome, recorded:** no change will be made to the quoted words. The person's words stay as they are, and the record keeps them because they are what makes the paper's claims about who decided what checkable.
