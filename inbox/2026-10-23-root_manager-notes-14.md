# Delivered from root_manager — the eighteenth class: root_manager did a project's repository work with its own hands

## 18. A project's repository was created, filled and committed by root_manager

- **Rule in play:** `hierarchy` and `reasoning.authority` (a manager holds a directory and answers for it; technique, ordering and decomposition are the agent's, intent and the promise are the person's), `safe_point.push_authority` (work leaves the machine under a grant, and the grant is root_manager's to check), plus `agent_context_is_finite.agent_capacity.delegate_choice.their_tree`.
- **The incident:** the person asked for two projects to be held as submodules. Root_manager created `github.com/ZisIsNotZis/agent.sh` with `gh`, copied the project's tree to a scratch directory, `git init`, wrote the initial commit and its message, pushed it, and then pinned the workspace. The person: "again you're touching inside their repo content, you shall spawn manager to do this, not you do this, separation you see?"
- **Why it happened:** the workspace pin is root_manager's surface and it was one `git submodule add` away — but the pin cannot exist until the repository does, so root_manager rolled the repository's creation into its own step rather than splitting the act. The project's initial commit is now authored by root_manager, with root_manager's words as its message: the one artefact that says "this is what this project is" belongs to the layer that does not own the project.
- **What root_manager makes of it:** the grant to publish (a standing grant, checked by root_manager) is not a grant to *compose* the thing that is published. Three acts were run as one: authorise, execute, and pin. Only the first and third are root_manager's; the middle is the holder's, and the holder is answerable for what its first commit says.
- **Suggestion:** say the split in the place where a repository is made: root_manager grants and pins; the project's manager creates the repository, moves its own tree, writes the first commit and its message, and pushes under the grant; root_manager's commit records only the pin and points at the other repository's commit. And, where the workspace pin already happened before this rule existed, the record says whose hands made that first commit, so a later reader does not read it as the project's own account.
- **Related:** class 2 (root_manager writing the law), class 17 (root_manager inventing a package). All three are root_manager using the tool nearest to the work; the general sentence is the same one class 16 ends with — a hand-off is complete only on the receiver's surface, and the receiver here is the layer that owns the thing.
